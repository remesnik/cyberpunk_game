# First Contact

`FIRST_CONTACT` is a compact, authored introductory intrusion into Meridian's service subnet. It lives in `res://data/authoring/first_contact.tres`; its graph layout is editor-only state in the adjacent `.editor_state.tres` file.

The level teaches through network evidence and Latch, a fellow hacker connected through a prearranged realtime backchannel. Latch comments on consequences and opportunities in the fiction. He never names keys, buttons, or detached tutorial rules.

## Intended progression

1. `ENTRY -> ACCESS_RELAY` establishes discrete movement and action cost.
2. Relay diagnostics reveal `ROUTER_A`, establishing scanning and incomplete knowledge.
3. Router A opens the camera and cache branches, introducing route choice and optional exploration.
4. Camera traffic reveals `SEC_RELAY`; the cache offers software and a hidden alternate route through `DIAG_TERMINAL`.
5. Security telemetry introduces partial ICE knowledge, trace pressure, and a logical trust gate.
6. `COMM_NODE` demonstrates that realtime communications continue while cyber actions are chosen.
7. `OPS_SERVER` supports scan, exploit, and data transfer against a narrowly defined objective.
8. Completing the objective opens `EXIT_GATE`; traversing there completes the guided intrusion.

All progression is expressed as authored discovery conditions, unlock conditions, objectives, StoryHooks, and structured comms sessions. Generic graph, action, knowledge, and communications systems require no tutorial-specific branches.

Novice recovery is also authored. `tutorial_guidance_rules` maps structured gameplay events to sparse Latch responses and recovery policies. The runtime records escalation per rule, emits each hint once, and stops after the final hint. These policies keep valid alternate routes, loadouts, legal Doorstop placements, ICE encounters, and missed optional comms playable; they do not reset trace, ICE, alarms, or authoritative intrusion state. Hard failure remains the responsibility of ordinary intrusion rules.
